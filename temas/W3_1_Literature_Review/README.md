## Detailed Reading: Constructing a Systematic Review

A literature review locates and summarizes prior studies on a specific topic. Its primary function is to share previous findings, relate the current study to the existing literature, and establish a reference point for comparing new results.

### Organizational Structures
The structure of a literature review depends on the research paradigm:
- **Qualitative Structure**: Used exploratorily when there is little prior research on the topic. The literature is incorporated inductively at the end of the study.
- **Quantitative Structure**: Used at the beginning of the study to justify research questions and hypotheses. It introduces the problem and presents theories that explain expected relationships.
- **Mixed-Methods Structure**: Adopts qualitative or quantitative approaches depending on the strategy. In sequential designs, the literature is presented in each phase according to the specific method applied.

### The Review Process
Conducting a review requires a preset protocol that specifies inclusion criteria, search strategies, and quality assessment procedures. This protocol reduces selection bias and increases reliability.

The execution consists of defining search terms (using thesauri), querying databases (e.g., JSTOR, Web of Science), and conducting forward and backward searches based on key authors. Articles are then prioritized through a screening process:
1. Identification of records via databases.
2. Removal of duplicates.
3. Screening of abstracts.
4. Full-text assessment for eligibility.
5. Final inclusion in the study.

### Literature Maps
A literature map is a visual tool that organizes the reviewed articles to show how the proposed study contributes to or expands existing research. Common formats include:
- **Hierarchical**: Presents literature from general topics down to the specific proposed study.
- **Flow-based**: Organizes literature sequentially from left to right.
- **Set-based**: Uses intersecting circles to represent bodies of literature, locating the proposed study in the intersections where research is needed.
