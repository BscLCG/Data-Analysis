# Week 4: The Use of Theory in Research

## Overview

This week explores the critical role that theory plays in shaping research. It examines how theories are identified, defined, and applied differently depending on whether the study employs a quantitative, qualitative, or mixed-methods design. The materials also discuss how to operationalize variables, establish causal relationships, and construct visual theoretical models.

## Key Concepts

- **Theory:** A set of interrelated constructs, definitions, and propositions that present a systematic view of a phenomenon to explain or predict outcomes.
- **Levels of Theory:** 
  - *Macro:* Large-scale social institutions over long periods.
  - *Meso:* Intermediate structures like organizations or communities.
  - *Micro:* Short-term, face-to-face interactions.
- **Types of Variables:**
  - *Independent / Predictor:* Variables that influence outcomes.
  - *Dependent / Response:* The outcomes being influenced.
  - *Mediating:* Variables that transmit the effect of an independent variable to a dependent variable.
  - *Moderating:* Variables that affect the strength or direction of a relationship.
  - *Confounding:* Unmeasured variables that may spuriously influence the outcome.
- **Theoretical Visual Models:** Diagrams connecting variables with directional arrows and signs (+/-) to map hypothesized causal relationships.

## Relevant Points

- **Quantitative Use of Theory:** Used deductively to test hypotheses. The theory provides a rigid framework at the beginning of the study to operationalize variables and predict outcomes.
- **Qualitative Use of Theory:** More flexible. It can be used deductively as a broad explanation, as a theoretical standpoint (e.g., critical or feminist theory) to guide data collection, or inductively as the endpoint/conclusion of the study.
- **Causality vs. Correlation:** Establishing causality requires more than statistical correlation; it demands a credible causal identification strategy and a robust research design (such as experiments) to control for confounding variables.
