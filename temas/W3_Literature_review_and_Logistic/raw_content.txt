Literature map

A literature map enables researchers to organise the literature review. 
It helps researchers understand how the proposed study adds to, expands upon, or replicates existing research.
In positivistic approaches it helps to map theoretical relationships between constructs.
1.
Hierarchical
Presenting the literature from the top down, ending with the proposed study at the bottom.
2.
Flow-based
Understands the literature from left to right, with the far-right section advancing towards a proposed study.
3.
Set-based
Circles represent a body of literature, and the intersection of circles indicates areas where further research is needed.

In a conservative approach, until proven otherwise, the null hypothesis is assumed to be valid. When analyzing a hypothesis test, we want to know:
What is the probability of obtaining results like those observed in the sample if the null hypothesis were actually true?
This probability is the p-value.
A p-value < 0.05 means that: there is only a 5% or lower probability that a result like the one observed in the sample would occur under the null hypothesis.
If this happens, we should reject the null hypothesis that there is no treatment effect and conclude that, given the available data, the treatment had an effect. In other words, the treatment effect was significantly different from zero.
If you see a p-value of 0.01, this does not mean that there is a 1% probability that the result is due to chance.

The p-value represents the probability of observing the results obtained, or more extreme results, assuming that the null hypothesis is actually true.

Why is the p‑value problematic and how can it lead to Type I errors?
We generally use α = 0.05 as the criterion for statistical significance, but this value is arbitrary, and it is important to understand how it came to be used.
Fisher made numerous fundamental contributions to statistics and developed the concept of significance testing, in which the p‑value is used as a guide to reach a conclusion about a hypothesis.
Ronald Fisher (1890-1962)
Fisher used the term “hypothesis” to refer specifically to the null hypothesis.
If p < 0.01, he generally regarded the result as clearly significant.
He used 0.05 as a reference point, but for a broader range of p‑values, possibly between 0.01 and 0.20, he often recommended additional experiments to further investigate the observed effect.
Fisher viewed the p‑value as a measure of the strength of evidence against the hypothesis: the smaller the p‑value, the stronger the reason to doubt the null hypothesis
How can we evaluate whether an observed result provides genuine evidence against a hypothesis, rather than being a coincidence produced by random variation?

Neyman and Pearson suggested that a p‑value < 0.05 implies that the data provide sufficient evidence to reject the null hypothesis and conclude that an effect is statistically significant.

⚠️ However, the use of the Neyman–Pearson significance criterion with α = 0.05 can be problematic and carries a high probability of producing Type I errors, unless certain conditions are met when designing the study and analysing the data:
The sample size must be determined in advance.
The hypotheses and the analysis used to test them must be defined beforehand.
Potential control variables must be identified prior to the analysis.
Criteria for excluding participants and handling missing data must be specified in advance.
For exploratory analyses (that is, analyses that are not hypothesis‑testing), a more stringent significance criterion should be used.

What happens when the sample size is not determined in advance?
Simmons, J. P., Nelson, L. D., & Simonsohn, U. (2011). False-Positive Psychology: Undisclosed Flexibility in Data Collection and Analysis Allows Presenting Anything as Significant. Psychological Science, 22(11), 1359–1366. https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797611417632
According to Simmons et al. (2011), failing to decide the sample size (or the rule for stopping data collection) before starting the study is an example of “researcher degrees of freedom”.
This practice, which involves collecting data based on preliminary analyses, can dramatically increase the actual false‑positive rate beyond the nominal 5% threshold (p ≤ 0.05).
For example, a researcher who begins with 10 observations per condition and tests for significance after adding each new observation finds a significant effect in 22% of cases.

This demonstrates that flexibility in choosing the sample size, especially stopping data collection once significance is reached, can unacceptably facilitate the publication of “statistically significant” evidence for a false hypothesis.

What is Zotero?
Zotero is a reference manager. It is designed to store, manage, and cite bibliographic references such as books and articles. In Zotero, each of these references constitutes an item. More broadly, Zotero is a powerful tool for collecting and organizing information and research sources, and for structuring literature review projects.
Each item in Zotero contains different metadata, depending on the type of item. Items may include books, articles, reports, web pages, artworks, films, letters, manuscripts, sound recordings, invoices, court cases, statutes, and many others.
Items appear in the central panel of Zotero. The metadata of each item is displayed in the right panel. This metadata includes titles, creators, editors, dates, page numbers, and any other information needed to cite the item. This allows you to have a complete and organized view of all relevant details of each item, making it easier to manage and reference your research sources.
Installation Process
After installing Zotero, you must install its browser connectors. The Zotero browser connector helps capture bibliographic information directly from your browser.
When you find a relevant source, such as an academic article, a book in a digital library, or a webpage, you can use the connector to automatically save item details to your Zotero library.
After installing Zotero, you must install its Word processor plugin. Among the different ways to automatically generate bibliographies (as well as in-text citations and footnotes), word processor plugins are the most powerful and easy to use. These plugins, available for Microsoft Word, LibreOffice, and Google Docs, create dynamic bibliographies: when you insert a new citation into your manuscript, the bibliography will automatically update to include the cited item.
General Overview of the Zotero UI
Using Zotero in Word
Citations
You can start citing with Zotero by clicking the “Add/Edit Citation” button in the Zotero menu. When you press the button, the citation dialog will appear.
The citation dialog is used to select items from your Zotero library and create a citation. To do this:
Start typing part of a title, the surname of one or more authors, and/or a year in the search box. Matching items will instantly appear below the dialog.
Matching items will appear for each library in your Zotero database (“My Library” and any groups you belong to). Items already cited in the document will appear at the top of the list under “Cited.”
Select an item by clicking on it or pressing Enter when it is highlighted. The item will appear in the dialog inside a shaded bubble. Press Enter again to insert the citation and close the “Add Citation” dialog.
In the “Add Citation” dialog, you can click on the bubble of a cited item and then click “Open in My Library (or Group Library name)” to view it in Zotero. Items that are orphans (not connected to anything in your Zotero database) will not have an “Open in My Library” button.
Bibliography
When you click the “Add/Edit Bibliography” button, a bibliography is inserted at the cursor location. As you use the plugin, Zotero will automatically update the bibliography based on the citations included in the document.
If you need to add items to the bibliography that you have not cited in the document, you can click the “Add/Edit Bibliography” button again to open the bibliography editor. Manual edits made directly in Word will be overwritten the next time Zotero updates the document.
Additionally, Zotero allows you to choose from a wide variety of citation styles to adapt your bibliography to the specific requirements of academic publications or institutions. If you need even more customization, you can use the citation style editor to adjust it as needed.
