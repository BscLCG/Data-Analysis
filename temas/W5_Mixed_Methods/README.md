# Week 5: Mixed Methods Procedures

## Overview

This week introduces the rationale and procedures for conducting Mixed Methods research. It details how the integration of quantitative (closed) and qualitative (open) data can provide a deeper, more robust understanding of a research problem than either method alone. The materials cover basic mixed methods designs, complex configurations, and the specific procedures for integrating and visualizing different types of data.

## Key Concepts

- **Mixed Methods Characteristics:** The collection, analysis, and integration of both quantitative and qualitative data within a single study or series of studies.
- **Data Types:**
  - *Open Data (Qualitative):* Information where predefined response options are not provided (e.g., interviews, observations).
  - *Closed Data (Quantitative):* Predefined response options (e.g., rating scales, test scores).
- **Integration:** The process of merging or connecting the two databases.
- **Joint Displays:** Tables or charts that present the side-by-side combination of the two data sources to facilitate comparison.
- **Meta-inferences:** Conclusions that emerge specifically from the integration of the two methods.
- **Basic Designs:**
  - *Convergent:* Merges both types of data concurrently to compare results and find convergence or divergence.
  - *Explanatory Sequential:* Collects quantitative data first, followed by qualitative data to help explain the initial statistical results.
  - *Exploratory Sequential:* Collects qualitative data first to explore a phenomenon, using the findings to build a quantitative instrument for broader testing.
- **Complex Designs:** Embedding mixed methods within larger frameworks such as experiments, case studies, evaluation processes, or participatory action research.

## Relevant Points

- **Added Value:** Mixing methods neutralizes the limitations of a single approach and leverages their respective strengths.
- **Sampling Strategy:** In sequential designs, the qualitative sample is often a subset of the quantitative sample (Explanatory), whereas in exploratory designs, the samples are drawn from the same population but typically consist of different individuals.
- **Design Selection:** The choice of design is dictated by the study's purpose—whether the researcher aims to compare findings, explain surprising statistical results, or develop a culturally specific measurement instrument.
