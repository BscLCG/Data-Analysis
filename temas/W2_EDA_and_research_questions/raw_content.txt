Interquartile Range (IQR)The interquartile range (IQR) is calculated using the limits of the data located between the first and third quartiles. The IQR is a more robust measure of dispersion than the variance or the standard deviation and should therefore be preferred for small or asymmetric distributions.

For symmetric distributions (not necessarily normal) and N > 30, results should be expressed as mean ± standard deviation.
For asymmetric distributions, N < 30, or when there is evidence of outliers, results should be expressed as median ± IQR, as these are more robust.

Bivariate Regression and Covariance
The connection between regression and covariance can be observed in a bivariate regression model (with regressor X and dependent variable Y). 

The slope and intercept of this regression correspond to the values of β0 and β1​ that minimise the residual sum of squares (RSS). 



The term RSS refers to a sum of squares because, when performing this minimisation on a particular sample, the expectation is replaced by a sample mean or sample sum.
An implication of this is that when two variables are not correlated (i.e., have a covariance of zero), the regression of either variable on the other yields a slope coefficient equal to zero.
