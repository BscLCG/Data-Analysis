## Detailed Reading: Mixed Methods Procedures

Mixed methods research consists of collecting, analyzing, and integrating quantitative and qualitative data within a single study. Researchers use this approach when a single methodology cannot fully address the research problem. Quantitative data captures magnitude and frequency but lacks context; qualitative data captures contextual mechanisms but cannot be generalized.

### Basic Designs
Implementing a mixed methods study requires selecting a specific structural design that dictates the timing and weighting of data collection:

1. **Convergent Design**: The researcher collects quantitative and qualitative data concurrently. The goal is to compare the two databases to identify convergence or divergence. For example, if a survey indicates high job satisfaction but interviews reveal structural frustration, the researcher analyzes this contradiction.
2. **Explanatory Sequential Design**: The study occurs in two phases. Phase one is quantitative. The researcher collects data, runs statistical models (such as [logistic regression](../W3_2_Logistic_Regression/README.md)), and identifies significant or unexpected results. Phase two is qualitative and specifically targets these results. The qualitative data explains the quantitative outcome. The qualitative sample must be a direct subset of the quantitative sample.
3. **Exploratory Sequential Design**: The study reverses the sequential order. Phase one is qualitative. The researcher explores a topic without predetermined variables, identifying core themes. Phase two uses those themes to build a quantitative instrument (e.g., a survey) for broader testing. In this design, the researcher uses different individuals from the same population for both phases to prevent the qualitative participants from skewing the subsequent quantitative test.

### Integration and Meta-Inferences
Having two separate datasets in a study does not constitute mixed methods research; the data must be integrated. Researchers merge the findings to generate meta-inferences—conclusions that emerge specifically from the combination of the two methods.

To achieve integration, researchers use specific tools:
- **Joint Displays**: Tables or matrices that map statistical results directly alongside qualitative quotes or themes, facilitating direct comparison.
- **Data Transformation**: Quantifying qualitative data by counting the frequency of a theme and applying statistical tests to those counts.

By explicitly integrating the data streams, researchers neutralize the limitations of isolated methods and provide empirical support for complex phenomena.
