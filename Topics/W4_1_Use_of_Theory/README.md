## Detailed Reading: Theory in Research Design

A theory provides a systematic view of phenomena by specifying the relationships among variables with the aim of explaining natural events. Its use varies depending on the research approach.

### Theory in Quantitative Designs
In quantitative research, theory is used deductively. It operates as an upfront framework that organizes the research questions and dictates data collection procedures. Theories explain how and why specific variables relate to one another.

#### Variable Definitions
Quantitative theoretical models rely on precisely defined variables:

- **Independent Variables (Predictors)**: Variables that influence or affect the outcomes.
- **Dependent Variables (Response)**: Outcomes influenced by the predictors.
- **Mediating Variables**: Variables that transmit the effect of an independent variable on a dependent variable, explaining the mechanism or process of the relationship.
- **Moderating Variables**: Variables that interact with independent variables, changing the direction or strength of the relationship.
- **Confounding Variables**: Unmeasured third variables that may independently influence the outcome, obscuring true causal relationships if not controlled.

#### Causal Links and Visual Models
Researchers construct causal models to represent these relationships. A visual model typically lists independent variables on the left, intervening/mediating variables in the center, and dependent variables on the right. Arrows indicate the direction of influence, and valence signs ($+$ or $-$) denote the hypothesized positive or negative impact. 

### Theory in Qualitative Designs
In qualitative research, theory is employed in three distinct ways:

1. **As a Deductive Explanation**: Ethnographic or anthropological studies often begin with established cultural themes (e.g., social control, kinship structures) that provide a framework of hypotheses to be examined through fieldwork.
2. **As a Theoretical Perspective (Standpoint)**: The theory provides general guidance concerning structural inequalities (e.g., feminist theories, critical race discourses). It shapes the questions, dictates data collection methods, and often includes a call for social action.
3. **As an Endpoint (Inductive)**: In approaches like grounded theory, the study begins with participant data, groups them into themes, and systematically builds up to a generalized model. Here, the theory is the final product, rather than the starting point.
